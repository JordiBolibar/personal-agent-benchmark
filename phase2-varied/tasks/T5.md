### T5 [glaciology theory]
Part A. Starting from the stress balance for a parallel-sided slab with slope α and Glen's flow law, derive the following under the shallow ice approximation, for general n:
- the horizontal velocity profile u(z)
- the surface velocity from deformation
- the depth-averaged velocity
- the flux
- the form of the diffusivity D

Then add Weertman-type sliding, u_b = C τ_b^m, and give the modified flux and diffusivity. Give the ratio between surface and depth-averaged velocity for no sliding, and say how sliding changes it.

Part B. A glacier point has H = 300 m, surface slope 0.05, ρ = 900 kg/m³, g = 9.81 m/s², n = 3, and A = 2.4e-24 Pa⁻³ s⁻¹. Observed surface speed is 40 m/a. Compute the deformation speed and the implied sliding speed in m/a, assuming zero noise.

Part C. Now assume H has a +10% error and the slope has a +20% error (independently). Estimate the effect of each on the inferred sliding speed.

Part D. Explain when A and sliding can or cannot be separated when you use only surface velocity and thickness data, and which extra data would help. Name at least three places in a real valley glacier where SIA fails, and say how each failure would bias an inversion.

Use headings A to D. Show the calculations.
