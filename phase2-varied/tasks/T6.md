### T6 [glaciology theory]
A valley glacier has mean thickness H = 150 m, length L = 6 km, terminus balance b_t = −5 m/a, a mean surface speed of 40 m/a, n = 3, and a mass balance gradient β = 0.007 a⁻¹ (ice equivalent, per metre of elevation).

1. Derive the volume response time τ_V ≈ H/(−b_t) from a simple mass budget, stating every assumption. Compute it.
2. Derive the kinematic wave speed from the SIA flux, and explain why it is larger than the mean ice speed. Estimate the kinematic time scale L/c and the diffusive time scale L²/D. Choose a sensible estimate of D and justify it.
3. Include the surface elevation feedback, ∂b/∂t = β ∂S/∂t, in a linear model for the mean thickness. Find how the effective response time changes. Find the condition under which the feedback leads to runaway growth or loss, and say why a bed slope and geometry changes matter.
4. You invert for a flow parameter using 10 years of observed dH/dt. Part of the observed thinning is the delayed response to climate before the observation period. Explain how this history biases the inferred parameter if the initial state is assumed to be in steady state, and give the sign of the bias if the glacier is really retreating. Propose two practical ways to reduce this bias.

Use numbered sections matching the items, with numbers where possible. End with a three-line summary of which time scale matters most for this glacier.
